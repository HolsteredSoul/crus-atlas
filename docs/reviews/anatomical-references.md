# Anatomy supplementation: evidence and acceptance criteria

Research review: 2026-09-07. This is an implementation reference, not clinical validation. Source names alone establish availability, not correct geometry. No paper's population-average measurements should be copied directly into this model's coordinates without identifying its corresponding landmarks.

## Tendon partitions (A–B)

Accept a partition only when every original evaluated face is retained exactly once across the corresponding muscle and tendon outputs, including EDL's existing separate tendon object. The assembled boundaries, source tendon colour regions, distal slips and interconnections must remain unchanged. Material labels establish a source-defined partition; they do not establish a histologically precise myotendinous junction.

For plantar verification, FDL crosses plantar to FHL around the knot of Henry. Interconnections vary and should be preserved if present, not invented to achieve an idealised pattern. Inspect the source through the whole route, particularly behind the medial malleolus, under the sustentaculum tali and across the plantar midfoot. [Beger et al., cadaveric morphometry of the knot of Henry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6136317/) includes dissection and connection-pattern figures. [Mao et al., 68-specimen anatomical study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5660160/) provides complementary plantar relationships.

## Interosseous membrane and distal interosseous ligament (C and G)

The shaft membrane and distal interosseous ligament are related but should not become two overlapping copies of one sheet. Preserve source vascular openings and inspect the interosseous margins of both bones in anterior, posterior and transverse views.

[Tano et al. (2021), anatomical, micro-CT and histological study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33983148/) identifies a proximal thickened ligament portion associated with a prominence on the medial distal fibula and nearby passage of the perforating fibular artery; the middle region includes thinner fibrofatty tissue. This contradicts modelling the entire distal interval as a uniform solid rectangular block. The [full article and figures](https://www.jbjs.org/reader.php?name=JBJS.20.01545.pdf&rsuite_id=2737161&type=pdf) are the preferred inspection reference; figures 2–5 relate bone morphology to soft tissue.

[Lilyquist et al. (2016), cadaveric analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1071100716643083) reports superior and inferior ligament attachments relative to distal tibial articular cartilage. These are population measurements for cross-checking scale, not this mesh's attachment coordinates. [Hermans et al., multimodality anatomy with dissections](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3039176/) provides complementary views of the syndesmotic structures.

**Authoring gate:** identify actual distal tibial/fibular attachment surfaces and the membrane transition in the source. A guessed distal slice of the membrane does not establish a separately segmented interosseous ligament. If those landmarks remain ambiguous, retain the verified membrane and record the distal ligament as unresolved.

## Plantar aponeurosis (D)

[Chen et al. (2014), cadaveric anatomy and biomechanics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3879302/) contains plantar dissections (figures 1–2), central digital bundles and their distal relationships. [Stecco et al. (2013), macroscopic and microscopic anatomy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3842207/) provides a complementary study of fascial structure and the calcaneal/Achilles region.

Check calcaneal attachment, central fan and source-supported digital branching in Sole and oblique views. The aponeurosis overlies intrinsic plantar muscles; it is not a duplicate FDB muscle/tendon surface. Do not extend five simple cords to distal toe tips or claim representation of all dermal/plantar-plate fibres if the source lacks them. Preserve the source's supported medial/lateral portions and describe its actual extent.

## Crural fascia and septa (E)

[Compartments of the crural fascia (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10625514/) combines ultrasound, dissection and histology with figures of fascial connections and distal compartments. [Quantitative analysis of intermuscular septa in the leg (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8296794/) documents variation in septal location using cross-sectional imaging.

Verify the anterior septum between anterior/lateral compartments, posterior septum separating the lateral compartment from posterior muscles, and transverse septum between superficial/deep posterior groups. A septum should connect its source fascial and bony boundaries without crossing a muscle belly arbitrarily. Inspect upper, middle and lower calf sections. Keep the existing convex-hull pressure envelopes separately labelled as derived illustrations, even when they share the fascia display colour.

## Tendon sheaths (F)

Use available source sheath objects and verify which tendons each encloses. Do not generate a complete uniform sleeve from muscle belly to insertion. The common EDL/fibularis-tertius sheath and common retromalleolar fibular sheath are not independent duplicate sheaths for each tendon. Retinacula and synovial sheaths are distinct structures.

The publisher's [Moore lower-limb chapter sample](https://downloads.lww.com/wolterskluwer_vitalstream_com/sample-content/9780781775250_Moore/samples/Moore6e-Chapter_5_LowerLimb.pdf) supplies labelled anterior ankle relationships, including the common EDL/FT sheath. [Tibialis anterior and extensor retinaculum: cadaver/patient MRI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16861505/) is a complementary source for TA's constrained ankle course.

[Peroneal tendoscopy, with eight-ankle anatomical study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3535154/) includes source dissections and thin mesotendineal structures within the sheath; keep the retromalleolar shared enclosure and distal diverging courses. The plantar fibularis-longus sheath should follow its cuboid turn, not continue with brevis toward the fifth metatarsal.

[Cadaveric FHL tendoscopy study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11733874/) shows the posterior ankle, sustentacular tunnel and knot-of-Henry regions (figure 1). Check FHL/FDL separation and crossing against this and the knot-of-Henry studies above. These references support course inspection; they do not validate an arbitrary sheath thickness or exact endpoint for every individual source mesh.

## Deltoid components (G)

Two complementary primary studies support authored candidates:

- [Campbell et al. (2014), quantitative cadaveric anatomy](https://sportsfootankle.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/ligament-anatomy-deltoid-complex-ankle-qualitative-quantitative-anatomical-study.pdf): figures 1–2 show dissected layers; figures 4–6 show footprints. Tibiospring is superficial, arising near the anterior colliculus and attaching to the spring ligament. Deep anterior tibiotalar lies beneath the superficial bands, with its talar attachment just below the trochlear cartilage. The study found variability among components.
- [Gregersen et al. (2022), cadaveric anatomy and function](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9201323/): independent dissections support these relationships, including tibiospring attachment around the posterior third of the spring ligament and the deep anterior band's medial talar-body insertion. Reported widths/thicknesses are plausibility checks, not an instruction to replace this model's morphology with averages.

**Authoring gate:** identify the anterior colliculus/intercollicular groove and medial talar articular border in the actual model before placing a deep anterior tibiotalar band. Tibiospring requires an identifiable spring-ligament receiving surface; a tibia-to-navicular strip is a different structure. If an appropriate source spring ligament exists, integrating that receiving surface is a necessary dependency. If it is absent or ambiguous, defer tibiospring instead of leaving it floating or falsely attaching it to bone. Keep authored additions separately selectable and labelled; do not relabel the resulting complex as a universally complete deltoid.

## Review limitations

The controlling agent's Blender inventory found `Posterior tibiotalar ligament.r`, `Plantar calcaneonavicular ligament.r` and `Calcaneonavicular ligament.r`, but no separately named anterior tibiotalar, tibiospring or interosseous tibiofibular ligament. All requested C–F source candidates were found. These are inventory findings, not acceptance of evaluated geometry. In particular, inspect whether the plantar calcaneonavicular candidate includes the superomedial spring surface required by tibiospring; the similar calcaneonavicular name is not interchangeable evidence.

For an authored G candidate, save surface-registered attachment annotations and compare both medial and oblique views to the reference footprint figures. Bounding-box-derived points alone fail that registration check. A missing source object is not itself a reason to exclude a candidate when the receiving anatomical landmarks can be identified and the authored reconstruction is explicitly disclosed.

This report inspected published descriptions, figure captions and accessible article content. Some PMC open calls returned an access challenge although indexed article text was available; the principal deltoid PDF was text-inspectable. It does not claim that every publication figure was visually reviewed or that the Blender source geometry has passed these criteria. Record the actual source objects and rendered checks separately in the batch evidence.

## D render and description review

Reviewed `images/blender-aponeurosis-sole.png` with the source aponeurosis over FDB, abductor hallucis and bones. The new geometry represents a broad central plantar sheet with a coarse transverse forefoot margin. The visible digital tendons beyond that margin belong to the underlying muscle layer in this composition; this image does not establish separately modelled digital aponeurosis slips. Therefore the initial insertion phrase “Source fan and digital slips” overclaims visible source detail.

Recommended insertion text: **“Plantar forefoot; distal digital attachments are not resolved in this source mesh.”** Recommended description: **“Source representation of the broad central plantar aponeurosis over the intrinsic muscles. Distal digital branching, dermal attachments and plantar-plate connections are not separately resolved.”** Avoid qualifying all omitted structures as microscopic: the omitted digital branching also includes macroscopic anatomy.

No blocking issue is visible for accepting the explicitly limited source central-sheet representation. It must not be presented as complete plantar aponeurosis anatomy or evidence that all distal attachment relationships have been reconstructed.

## E section review

Reviewed the refreshed `images/blender-fascia-150-section.png`, `images/blender-fascia-280-section.png` and `images/blender-fascia-390-section.png` with flat, coloured review-only muscle/bone caps. These caps improve tissue identification and are not additions to the exported anatomical surfaces.

At an overview level, the fascial sleeve follows the external muscle envelope. The mid-calf section displays a boundary between the broad superficial posterior muscle mass and deeper tissue toward the tibia/fibula, with lateral septal boundaries around the fibular muscle group. Across the three cuts, no obvious gross septal sheet traversing a bone or central muscle belly is apparent. This supports retaining the source fascia/septa with the current limited descriptions.

The cuts do not establish sealed compartment topology, exact proximal/distal attachment continuity, fascial thickness or individual neurovascular apertures. Small gaps at interfaces cannot be confidently classified from these renders alone. The source sheets must remain distinct from illustrative pressure envelopes and must not be described as watertight, complete compartments.

## F sheath review and pre-existing retinaculum issue

Reviewed `images/blender-sheaths-foot.png`, `images/blender-sheaths-sole.png` and all eight sheath entries in `assets/supplements.json`. The blue sheaths are distinguishable from tendons, bone and other connective tissues. They occupy limited ankle/plantar segments rather than entire muscle-to-insertion sleeves. The plantar fibularis-longus segment curves across the cuboid region rather than continuing with brevis toward the fifth metatarsal. No gross new sheath-routing defect is apparent in these two views; individual enclosed tendon surfaces and exact endpoints are not fully visible.

The rose-purple pre-existing object labelled superior fibular retinaculum raises a separate anatomical flag: its broad anterior/dorsal ankle span resembles an extensor-retinacular pattern. A superior fibular retinaculum should retain the fibular tendons behind the lateral malleolus. Therefore the upstream name alone cannot support its current label. Inspect source components and other retinacula, bind a verified retromalleolar component if available, or clearly quarantine the unresolved representation. This is a source-label/location concern, not evidence that the new tendon sheaths caused it.

Metadata recommendations: remove Blender Skin/Subdivision terminology from the EHL user-facing description and retain that processing detail in provenance instead. Assign references by relevance: Moore for general sheath relationships, the TA imaging study for TA, the FHL study for FHL and the fibular study for fibular sheaths. Copying the fibular and FHL references to every anterior sheath suggests supporting evidence those studies do not provide.

## G candidate review, initial views

Reviewed `images/blender-ligament-candidates-medial.png`, `-oblique.png`, `-posterior.png`, `ligament-candidate.json` and `retinacula-inventory.json`. The orange plantar calcaneonavicular candidate is a short, thin plantar strap (18 vertices, 16 polygons), with no identifiable superomedial receiving wall in these views. Its name does not establish the tibiospring attachment surface required by the reference anatomy. Do not author tibiospring by attaching it to this strap solely because both are called spring-related structures.

The anterior tibiotalar and distal interosseous attachment surfaces are partly occluded by existing ligaments/assembled bones in these initial renders. These views alone therefore do not prove that registration is impossible. Bone-only medial/oblique views and a view of the opposing distal tibial/fibular surfaces were requested before deciding those two candidates.

For the disputed retinaculum, the source audit reports one connected mesh spanning both sides/anterior ankle; no clean independently disconnected superior-fibular component was found. Preserve the historical identifier for compatibility but use a neutral disputed-source label, remove confident fibular attachment/function claims and hide it by default. The evidence also does not justify silently relabelling it as a verified extensor retinaculum.

The subsequent bone-only `images/blender-medial-footprints-medial.png` and `-oblique.png` remove ligament occlusion. The medial malleolus and talar body are visible, but the collicular separation/intercollicular groove cannot be reproducibly identified at this surface detail, and the coarse cartilage border does not establish the small deep-anterior-tibiotalar talar footprint. A generally positioned ribbon would still have guessed attachment boundaries. Defer this authored band until a source-specific footprint annotation or better-resolved reference mesh permits registration; this does not imply that educational ligament authoring is impossible in general.

## G final disposition

Reviewed `images/blender-distal-footprint-fibula-only.png` and `-tibia-only.png` after requesting removal of the obscuring neighbouring bone. These expose the actual facing surfaces. Above its articular region, the source fibula has a smooth contour without a reproducibly identifiable interosseous-ligament prominence/footprint boundary. The opposing tibial view supplies a broad coarse contour and cartilage regions, not distinguishable ligament attachment limits. Together with `images/blender-distal-interval-section.png`, these images do not establish the thickened/fibrofatty ligament transition described by Tano et al. A generic filled gap or distal membrane slice would therefore introduce guessed boundaries.

Published descriptions support the general anatomy but do not supply these mesh-specific targets. No authored reconstruction of these three structures is accepted, and batch G must be reported as evaluated/deferred rather than reconstructed. Independent accepted source additions can be delivered under the user's explicit unresolved-candidate policy.
